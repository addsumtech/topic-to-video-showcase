Films made from a single request

Each film began as one request in a chat. topic-to-video researched the subject, wrote and voiced the script, designed every shot and rendered the video. No frame was animated by hand.

Explainers

Science and how-things-work films: one question, answered with the real mechanism, every number traced to a source.

Why a diamond sparkles

Explainer, Director · Blender 3D, Chinese, 1:44

Light bends as it enters the stone, a critical angle of about 24° traps it, two reflections off the back facets send it out the top, and dispersion splits it into colour. Every stone is rendered in Blender; the coloured fire is real dispersion: the colour shots are each rendered three times, at diamond’s red, green and blue refractive indices.

Why a falling cat lands on its feet

Explainer, Director, Chinese, 2:03

A cat has nothing to push against in the air, yet it turns over: it folds into two halves and twists them in turn, so its total rotation stays zero (the 1969 two-cylinder model). Drawn as a risograph print: spot inks, halftone and paper grain.

Why the sky is blue and sunsets are red

Explainer, Director, Chinese, 1:37

Rayleigh scattering sends short wavelengths across the whole sky; why the sky is not violet; and why the long, slanting path of evening light leaves only orange and red.

How a lens makes a sharp image

Explainer, Director, English, 2:01

One point of light, a pinhole, glass that slows one side of a beam, a cone of rays meeting again behind the lens, and why only one distance can be exactly sharp.

Why a torn QR code still scans

Explainer, Director, Chinese, 1:45

Codewords, Reed–Solomon error correction and the finder marks, shown on one real version-2 code built for the film. Every tear it shows, the one that still scans and the ones that do not, was checked with a real decoder.

How an MRI scanner makes a picture

Explainer, Director · Blender 3D, English, 2:07

No X-rays: up to 60% of you is water, each hydrogen proton is a tiny magnet wobbling about 64 million times a second at 1.5 tesla, a radio pulse at that pitch tips them over, and the echo they give back becomes the picture. Each row of a real brain slice sings its own pitch, and the chord is split back into rows to rebuild the slice. The scanner is rendered in Blender.

How noise cancelling works

Explainer, Remotion, English, 2:43

Sound as squeezed and stretched air, the flipped wave that cancels it, the two microphones in an earbud and the tiny window of time they have to work in.

job-hunt launch film

Product launch, Director, Chinese, 2:14

job-hunt, an open-source job-search skill for Claude Code and Codex, runs a whole job search, and every line it writes into your CV traces back to something you actually did: a thread from its logo ties each line to its source, and made-up ones are struck out. The Claude Code, Codex and shell windows are recorded from the real programs; the postings and CV on screen are demo content.

How they are made

Director
The default: research and script first, the voice recorded before any picture, one look chosen for the whole film, built sequence by sequence as web pages captured in headless Chrome, with real 3D from Blender where the subject needs it.
Remotion
Each shot is a React component rendered frame by frame, picked from a library of 227 shot designs or built for the film; heavier 3D shots rendered in Blender.
HTML
Each beat is a web page built for the film, animated in headless Chrome and captured frame by frame. Chrome is all it needs.
